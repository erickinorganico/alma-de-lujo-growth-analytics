## Research hypotheses and discriminating evidence

1. **Entry product:** socks might require a smaller initial commitment than full sportswear outfits. This is a hypothesis about actual landed unit cost, size complexity and purchase behavior; current costs are invented. Resolve using supplier quotes and an approved small pilot, not the synthetic margin.
2. **Color preference:** requests may differ by color, but content exposure and availability can confound preference. Record shown colors, responses, missing answers and stock at exposure. A preference survey estimates stated intent among responders, not demand or sales.
3. **Studio channel:** Pilates studios may offer relevant access to an audience. No partner was contacted. Before a contact or placement pilot, define commission, inventory ownership, exposure denominator, return responsibility and closing criteria.
4. **Bundling:** socks plus a garment could affect order value and contribution. Discounts, variable costs, returns and substitution must be measured; a higher basket value alone does not establish incremental profit.

## First research sequence

Owner discovery -> confirmed assortment/cost/operating constraints -> baseline measurement -> predeclared experiment -> approved execution -> reconciliation -> descriptive outcome -> causal claim only if design permits. With no real baseline, the experiment status stays DRAFT_APPROVAL_REQUIRED; no synthetic result promotes a business decision.

The versioned designs in generated `report.json` (`experiments`) include primary metric, guardrail, window, population, closure and evidence. The color survey has an explicitly exploratory minimum of 30 responses; this is a pragmatic reporting threshold, not a power calculation. The vitrine test requires owner-approved MDE and sample size before launch.
